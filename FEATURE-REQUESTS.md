<h1>How Feature Requests Work</h1>

<p>Thank you for thinking about how MoneyBug could be better. Here's how requests are handled, so you know what to expect.</p>

<h2>MoneyBug is kept small on purpose</h2>

<p>Everyone wants something a little different from their invoicing software. One person needs purchase orders, another needs retainers, another needs a report their accountant asked for. Each of these makes sense on its own, and while designing MoneyBug it became clear the app could grow in every direction to fit each person who uses it.</p>

<p>Every feature has a cost, though, even for people who never use it: another setting, another screen, more to learn and more that can go wrong. MoneyBug's job is to make tracking time and sending invoices quick and calm for everyone who uses it, and that means saying no to many good ideas.</p>

<h2>What happens to your request</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Every request is read.</li>
  <li>Some fit MoneyBug and are planned for a future update. Many won't make it into the app, and that's not a judgment of the idea or of how you work.</li>
  <li>There are no promises or timelines. I'm one person, and I'd rather do a few things well.</li>
  <li>A request may be closed with a short note on why. It stays visible, and others can still add to it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What makes a request most useful</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Describe the problem, not just the solution.</strong> "My client needs every invoice to show their PO number" tells me more than "add a PO field", and the answer may turn out to be something the app already does.</li>
  <li><strong>Say how you work today.</strong> What you do now, how often it comes up, and what it costs you in time or mistakes.</li>
  <li><strong>Check for an existing request first.</strong> If someone has already asked, add your situation to theirs rather than opening a new one: several people describing the same need carries more weight than a single request.</li>
  <li><strong>Keep it to one idea per request.</strong> It's easier to follow and discuss.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What MoneyBug doesn't aim to be</h2>

<p>To save you time, these are outside what MoneyBug sets out to do:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Accounting software.</strong> MoneyBug tracks time and invoices; bookkeeping, bank feeds and tax filing belong in an accounting app. That includes tracking your own business spending: MoneyBug bills expenses to a client as part of a job, but it isn't an expense tracker for receipts and categories.</li>
  <li><strong>A payment tool.</strong> MoneyBug makes the invoice; your client pays it however you usually get paid, and you mark it paid. It doesn't take payments, pay in bulk, or match bank deposits to invoices.</li>
  <li><strong>A reporting suite.</strong> The Dashboard and each client's page show your time and money at a glance; custom reports and deep analysis are an accounting app's or a spreadsheet's job.</li>
  <li><strong>A project manager.</strong> Jobs in MoneyBug are for billing the work, not planning it: no tasks, deadlines, schedules, approvals or file sharing.</li>
  <li><strong>A personal productivity tracker.</strong> MoneyBug times work so you can bill it. It isn't for tracking your day, your schedule, where your time goes or how productive you are: there are apps made for that.</li>
  <li><strong>A team tool.</strong> MoneyBug is for one person's own work, synced across their own Macs. It doesn't manage staff, contractors or payroll.</li>
</ul>
